Pediatric Difficult Airway Predictors

▼
⚠️
No single validated scoring tool exists for pediatric difficult airway prediction. Unlike adults, LEMON has limited applicability in infants and young children. Physical exam findings, syndromic history, and acquired conditions are your primary guides. Any predictor below should prompt PART activation before the first attempt.
📏 Physical Exam Predictors
FindingHow to AssessWhy It Matters
Micrognathia
Most important predictor in infants/non-cooperative children		 Lateral profile view — assess mandibulo-maxillary discrepancy. Chin recessed behind lower lip in profile. Reduced submandibular space → tongue cannot be displaced anteriorly → larynx cannot be visualized. Classic in Pierre Robin, Treacher Collins, Stickler.
Thyromental Distance
Most important measurable predictor with micrognathia		 Distance from thyroid notch to mentum (chin tip) with neck extended. Normal >3 fingerbreadths / >6 cm. Short TMD = inadequate submandibular space. In infants, <1.5–2 cm is concerning. Highly predictive of poor Cormack-Lehane view.
Mallampati Score
Useful only in cooperative children ≥4 yrs		 Seated, mouth fully open, tongue protruded. Class III/IV = uvula not visible or only base visible. In children >4 yrs, Mallampati III/IV has 43% positive predictive value for poor laryngoscopic view (Cormack-Lehane III/IV). Cannot reliably assess in infants or uncooperative children.
Mouth Opening / Inter-incisor Gap 3-finger test — can patient open mouth to ≥3 fingerbreadths? Normal ≥3 cm. Limited opening from restricted TMJ (Treacher Collins, Apert) or scarring. May prevent blade insertion entirely.
Neck Extension / Mobility Can child achieve full extension? Note any torticollis, surgical scars, hard collar. Fused cervical vertebrae (Klippel-Feil), atlantoaxial instability (Down syndrome), trauma. Severely limits sniffing position.
Macroglossia Tongue proportionally large relative to oral cavity. Assess tongue protrusion vs. lip line. Physically obstructs laryngoscopy view. Classic in Beckwith-Wiedemann, Down syndrome. Often worsens with sedation.
Tonsillar / Adenoid Hypertrophy Mallampati assessment, prior history of OSA, snoring, mouth breathing. Soft tissue obstruction during bag-mask ventilation. Particularly relevant in children 3–8 yrs. Worsens with muscle relaxation.
Obesity / Short Neck BMI, visual neck assessment, ear-to-sternal-notch alignment. Ramp position critical. Reduced functional residual capacity → faster desaturation. Short neck = less submandibular compliance.
🧬 Syndromic & Congenital High-Risk Conditions
ℹ️
Children with known syndromes below should be presumed difficult until proven otherwise. Activate PART early, plan for video laryngoscopy as primary, and have fiberoptic bronchoscopy and LMA available before any attempt.
SyndromePrimary Airway ProblemKey Management Pearl
Pierre Robin Sequence~1:8,500 births Triad: micrognathia + glossoptosis + cleft palate. Retrognathia pushes tongue posteriorly. Mask ventilation also difficult — small chin prevents seal. Prone positioning may improve oxygenation pre-intubation. Video laryngoscopy first-line. Fiberoptic available. PART before any attempt. Often requires NICU-level airway management at birth.
Treacher Collins SyndromeMandibulofacial dysostosis Mandibular hypoplasia + limited mouth opening + restricted TMJ + relative macroglossia + glossoptosis + OSA. Zygomatic and maxillary bone hypoplasia compounds view. VL with revised J-shaped stylet described in literature (240-case series). Fiberoptic standby. Expect difficult BVM — two-person technique. PART activation mandatory.
Goldenhar SyndromeHemifacial microsomia Unilateral mandibular hypoplasia → facial asymmetry complicates blade positioning and mask seal. Cervical vertebral anomalies common. Asymmetry means blade placement must be adjusted. VL preferred. Assess neck films pre-procedure if possible — cervical anomalies in ~30%.
Down SyndromeTrisomy 21 Macroglossia + atlantoaxial instability + subglottic stenosis + short neck + hypotonia. Multiple compounding factors. Avoid excessive neck flexion/extension — atlantoaxial instability risk. Use smaller ETT (subglottic stenosis — size down 0.5 mm). Macroglossia worsens with relaxation.
Apert / Crouzon / Pfeiffer SyndromesCraniosynostosis Craniosynostosis + midface hypoplasia → narrowed nasal and oropharyngeal passages. Proptosis in Crouzon. Choanal stenosis common. Nasal intubation may be impossible (choanal stenosis). Midface hypoplasia impairs mask seal. VL + fiberoptic plan. ENT on standby.
Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome Macroglossia — tongue physically obstructs laryngeal view. Tongue may be so large it protrudes from mouth at rest. Macroglossia worsens dramatically with muscle relaxation. Consider awake fiberoptic intubation in severe cases. Size up ETT — these children often large.
Klippel-Feil Syndrome Fused cervical vertebrae → severely limited or absent neck extension. Cannot achieve sniffing position. VL or fiberoptic intubation required — direct laryngoscopy nearly impossible without neck extension. Avoid forced extension — spinal cord injury risk.
CHARGE Syndrome Choanal atresia (bilateral = immediately life-threatening in neonates) + tracheomalacia + subglottic stenosis + cranial nerve deficits. Choanal atresia: place oral airway immediately at birth. Oral intubation only. Size down ETT for subglottic stenosis.
Stickler Syndrome Micrognathia + cleft palate — frequently underlies Pierre Robin Sequence. Hypermobile joints. Treat as Pierre Robin airway precautions. Ophthalmology involvement — high myopia/retinal detachment risk relevant to positioning.
🚨 Acquired & Emergency Predictors
ConditionAirway ThreatKey Consideration
EpiglottitisRapidly progressive supraglottic edema — can close airway within minutesOR with ENT if any time allows. Avoid agitating child. Inhalational induction preferred if conscious. VL + fiberoptic + surgical team standby.
Croup (Severe)Subglottic edema — may prevent passage of correctly sized ETTSize down ETT by 0.5–1 mm. Heliox while preparing. Dexamethasone + racemic epi first if time allows.
Retropharyngeal / Peritonsillar AbscessPosterior pharyngeal bulge distorts anatomy, may rupture on intubationAvoid blind nasal intubation. VL to visualize anatomy. ENT at bedside. Rupture risk = aspiration emergency.
AngioedemaRapid mucosal swelling — may progress to complete obstructionEarly intubation before edema progresses. Awake fiberoptic if progressive. Epinephrine IM immediately.
Burns / Inhalation InjuryProgressive airway edema over hours — window for intubation closesIntubate early if facial burns, singed nasal hairs, carbonaceous sputum, hoarseness. Do not wait for complete obstruction.
Foreign BodyPartial or complete obstruction — location determines approachAvoid BVM if complete obstruction (may push FB distally). Rigid bronchoscopy often required. ENT/Pulm on standby.
Facial / Neck TraumaHemorrhage, edema, disrupted anatomy, C-spine precautionsBlood in airway = poor view. Suction before every attempt. Inline stabilization for C-spine. VL preferred. Low threshold for surgical airway.
ObesityRapid desaturation + difficult BVM + limited neck extensionRamp position mandatory. Two-person BVM. HFNC pre-ox. Weight-based dosing (actual body weight for succinylcholine; consider lean body weight for other agents).

Miller vs Macintosh Blade

Pediatric blade selection by age and anatomy
💡
Recommendation: Miller preferred <2 years — anterior larynx, omega epiglottis. Mac acceptable from ~2–3 years and familiar to adult-trained providers. Video laryngoscopy preferred for all anticipated difficult airways at any age.

🟢 Miller (Straight)

SizeAge / Weight
Miller 0Premature / Newborn (<3 kg)
Miller 0Newborn–3 months (3–5 kg)
Miller 13 months–2 years (5–12 kg)
Miller 22–6 years (12–20 kg)
Miller 3>6 years / adult
Technique: Tip under epiglottis → direct lift. Best for anterior larynx and floppy omega epiglottis. Preferred <2 yrs by most pediatric airway experts. Requires precise lateral control.

🔵 Macintosh (Curved)

SizeAge / Weight
Mac 1Infant (rarely used)
Mac 22–8 years (12–25 kg)
Mac 38–14 years / small adult
Mac 4Large adolescent / adult
Technique: Tip into vallecula → indirect lift via hyoepiglottic ligament. Familiar to adult-trained ED providers. Acceptable from 2–3 years onward.

Bougie — Primary Stylet Protocol

▼
🔑
Bougie = Primary Stylet — every intubation. Load before every attempt. Replace the rigid stylet as your default. Gives immediate tactile feedback with a rescue tool already in position.
Patient SizeBougie TypeNotes
<3 kg (neonate)5 Fr peds bougie or 3.0 ETT styletMost neonatal intubations without bougie — use if view is poor
3–15 kgPediatric bougie 5–8 Fr / 55 cmFeel for tracheal clicks, carina hold-up ~10–15 cm from lip
15–30 kgPediatric bougie 10 Fr / 65 cmSame clicks + hold-up technique
>30 kgAdult bougie 15 Fr / 70 cmStandard adult technique
ℹ️
Technique tip: Pass bougie under direct or video view. Tracheal rings feel like speed bumps. Carina hold-up at 10–15 cm from lip in small children. Railroad ETT over bougie. Rotate tube 90° counterclockwise if resistance at cords.

Escalation Algorithm — Pediatric Respiratory Failure

▼
Step 1: Supplemental O₂ — NC / NRB · Target SpO₂ ≥94%
↓ SpO₂ goal not met or WOB increasing
Step 2: HFNC (1–2 L/kg/min) or Bubble CPAP (PEEP 5 cmH₂O)
↓ Persistent distress · O₂ requirement >50% needed
Step 3: BiPAP / NIPPV · DSI if agitated · Add Heliox if obstruction
↓ Failing NIPPV · Apnea · Hemodynamic instability · GCS ≤8
Step 4: RSI + Endotracheal Intubation · Activate PART before attempting

💉

Needle Cricothyrotomy — Full Technique

▼
⚠️
Needle cric provides temporary oxygenation only. CO₂ retention occurs rapidly — plan definitive airway within 30–45 min. In complete obstruction use I:E ≥ 1:8 to prevent barotrauma.
Materials
12–14g angiocath (child ≥5 yrs)18–20g angiocath (infant/small child) 10 mL syringe + saline3-way stopcock or Y-connector BVM + 3.0 ETT adapterWall O₂ / H-cylinder Jet ventilator (if available)Betadine prep
  1. Extend neck, identify CTM — Shoulder roll. Palpate between thyroid and cricoid. In infants CTM is ~2.6×3.0 mm — ultrasound if time permits.Use 18–20g catheter in infants; 12–14g in older children.
  2. Prep skin — Betadine or CHG (avoid CHG <2 months). Do not delay CICO management for prep.
  3. Insert needle — Angiocath on syringe with saline. 45° angle, caudally, midline. Constant aspiration.Air bubbles in syringe = tracheal position confirmed. Stop when air freely aspirates.
  4. Advance catheter, remove needle — Once air confirmed, advance plastic catheter into trachea. Remove needle while stabilizing catheter.⚠ Do not advance needle after catheter seated — posterior tracheal wall perforation risk.
  5. Reconfirm position — Aspirate air freely through catheter with syringe.
  6. Connect oxygen
    Option A — BVM adapter: 3.0 ETT connector → catheter hub → BVM. Ventilate 12–20 BPM (infant) / 10–15 BPM (child).
    Option B — Jet ventilation: Y-connector → 50 psi O₂. 1 sec ON / 4 sec OFF (partial obstruction). 1 sec ON / 8 sec OFF (complete obstruction).
  7. Secure and monitor — Manually stabilize catheter at all times. Watch chest rise. Monitor SpO₂.Subcutaneous emphysema = reassess position immediately.
  8. Call for definitive airway now — Pediatric Surgery / ENT / OR. Convert to surgical tracheostomy <30–45 min.
⚠️
Complications: Subcutaneous emphysema · Posterior tracheal perforation · Barotrauma / bilateral pneumothorax (highest risk in complete obstruction) · Catheter displacement · CO₂ retention
🔪

Surgical Cric (≥12 yrs) — Scalpel-Finger-Bougie

▼
  1. Declare CICO, position, ID membrane
  2. Stabilize larynx — Thumb + middle finger on thyroid alae, index on CTM
  3. Horizontal stab incision — Scalpel #20 through skin and CTM, 1–2 cm
  4. Hook finger caudally into incision — confirm tracheal lumen
  5. Insert bougie caudally — tracheal rings + carina hold-up at 26–30 cm
  6. Railroad ETT over bougie — 6.0–7.0 cuffed ETT sized per patient
  7. Confirm and ventilate — Inflate cuff. ETCO₂ waveform + bilateral breath sounds. Secure tube.
  8. Plan tracheostomy — Contact surgical team. Convert within 24 hours.

Attempt limitNo more than 3 laryngoscopy attempts before escalating to advanced techniques. Immediately escalate if hypoxia develops at any point — do not wait for attempt #3.
Roles at bedsideDesignated intubator · Backup intubator (PART member) · Airway assistant · Medication nurse · Team leader (calls the plan and transitions)
Post-intubationConfirm ETCO₂ waveform + CXR · Initiate post-intubation sedation (see Dosing tab) · Arrange PICU/NICU transfer as indicated
